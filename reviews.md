Reviewer 1 review score 4/5. Review. Summary: This submission uses a browser-based autoencoder and four guided experiments to explain how sparse features can share fewer dimensions. Strengths: I liked how the guided experiments keep the explanation tied to the model's behavior. Comparing dense and sparse inputs, then activating opposing features, gives readers a useful way to examine the tradeoff between capacity and interference. The experiments worked as described in my runs, and the article is clear about the limits of the toy model.

Suggestions:
* I would have liked to reach the experiments sooner. A short preview or shortcut would help with the long mathematical introduction.
* An option to load a converged example would help when the final experiment does not produce the expected pentagon.
* I would welcome a guided 3D example, since the abstract emphasizes this capability.
* The loss labels and small figure text could be clearer.


Reviewer 2 review score 4/5. Review: The introductory visualization is interesting, especially the part where we can see how changes to the input neurons affect the embeddings, but

* I would have liked to see more explanation near the visualization, as it is confusing on its own.
* The scrolly-telly visualization showing the mapping from feature space to embedding space and back is well done, but I wondered whether this section could be simplified, as it got into details, such as the Gram matrix, that are not strictly necessary for explaining this concept.

The final visualizations with the models and training were the most well done and helped me understand the introductory visualization. Overall Evaluation: Possibly Accept: I would argue for accepting this paper. Expertise: Knowledgeable.
