Maximum number of unit vectors that can be simultaneously placed in a d-dimensional space (d = 3 to 15) such that every pair subtends an angle of at least α degrees. The x-axis runs from 90° (left) to 63° (right); the y-axis uses a logarithmic scale. At 90° each dimension d can hold exactly 2d vectors (the cross-polytope); as the angle threshold decreases the achievable count rises in discrete steps, with higher-dimensional spaces (yellow) accommodating substantially more vectors than lower-dimensional ones (green). This illustrates why superposition—representing more features than available dimensions—becomes increasingly viable as dimensionality grows. Data combines closed-form solutions for regular simplices and cross-polytopes with numerically optimal spherical codes from N. J. A. Sloane’s tables (spherical-codes.org).
